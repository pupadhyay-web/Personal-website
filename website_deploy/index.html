About me

Experimental and computational aerosol scientist

I trained as a chemical engineer at IIT Bombay and found aerosol science through a joint master’s with Washington University in St. Louis, where I built an acoustic levitator and an electrodynamic balance to hold droplets in mid-air.

Now, in Prof. Rajan K. Chakrabarty’s AIR Lab, I build photoacoustic spectrometers to measure aerosol light absorption, and I simulate particle optics, aerosol dynamics, chemistry and radiative transfer. I use both to quantify the climate impacts of alumina from stratospheric aerosol injection and satellite re-entry.

Is alumina really a non-absorbing SAI particle?

Key result

Al2O3 absorption index k spans 1.4×10−4 to 1.2×10−3 across 375–1047 nm, measured in situ.

  • Agrees with single-particle EELS; absorption attributed to oxygen-vacancy defects (mid-gap state ≈ 240 nm).
  • Climate model simulations of an established scenario reveal that shortwave absorption has negligible effects on radiative forcing and stratospheric warming.

Are climate models missing SAI aggregate physics?

Current climate models treat solid aerosols as either modal or monodisperse particles.
Key result

Polydisperse monomers improve shortwave radiative efficiency: ≈5–25% (median), while aggregates of monodisperse monomers lose ≈15–20%.

  • Implemented diffusion-limited cluster–cluster aggregation (DLCA) to build Al2O3 aggregates from 28 monomer size distributions; fractal dimensions of 1.74–2.03 agree with the literature.
  • Computed their optics with the multi-sphere T-matrix method in the 14 shortwave bands that climate models use.
  • Ran 1-D radiative transfer (libRadtran, 16-stream DISORT) for a 4 Tg layer at 18–23 km.
  • Built a framework that quantifies the error in radiative forcing from treating polydisperse aerosols as monodisperse.

Sharper photoacoustic absorption measurements

Key results

Laser-drift correction lowers the detection limit from 8.7 to 7.5 Mm−1 (≈14%) for 1 s measurements with a 405 nm photoacoustic spectrometer.

COMSOL finite-element model of the resonator: sensitivity is set mainly by resonator diameter D and acoustic-filter opening Dh (optimum for L = 1 m: D ≈ 1.18 cm, Dh = 1 cm).

  • Referencing the signal to laser power cuts noise 13–16% at all averaging times.
  • Allan-deviation analysis confirms the correction removes drift bias in the background signal (random-walk noise unchanged).
Shine a laser on soot and it hums. That’s not a metaphor; that’s my PhD.

Tutorial: a photoacoustic spectrometer to measure aerosol light absorption

A practical guide to building and running a photoacoustic spectrometer, a first-principles way to measure aerosol light absorption in situ.

  • Walks through instrument construction, signal processing, calibration and detection limits, along with the technique’s inherent limitations.
  • Lays out the design trade-offs: laser power, resonator length and diameter, and Helmholtz-resonator acoustic filters that suppress background noise.

Master’s Research

Joint Master’s · IIT Bombay × WashU2021–2022

Contact-free levitation of droplets & aerosols

  • Built a non-resonant, single-axis acoustic levitator (Arduino-driven) that traps droplets at standing-wave nodes; simulated node shifts with frequency and phase.
  • Built a quadrupole electrodynamic balance with a droplet generator; MATLAB modeling of multi-droplet dynamics yielded a DC-bias method that offsets gravity for sharper imaging.
  • Modeled electrostatic scavenging of a 10 µm carbon particle by a charged 50 µm droplet: Coulomb attraction lifted the collection efficiency to 1.69.
  • Proposed a humidity-controlled chamber for studying how levitated pollen grains burst.

What’s next

A postdoc where measurements meet Earth system models

I’m looking for a group where I can learn global climate modeling from the inside and bring my aerosol optics expertise to the lab, building instruments and models side by side so that lab-constrained particle physics improves climate model predictions.

Direction 01

Fast, realistic aerosol optics for climate models

Speed up optical and radiative transfer codes, and extend my DLCA + T-matrix framework with DDA for irregular and coated particles, so realistic aerosol optics fit within a global climate model’s computational budget.

Builds on: my DLCA + T-matrix framework, DDA and libRadtran/DISORT.

Direction 02

Re-entry physics, from the lab to the stratosphere

Refine my first-order re-entry estimates with laboratory experiments that recreate ablation, and use them to sharpen the physics of re-entry, from the ablating surface and plume chemistry to the particles that climate models need to represent.

Builds on: the re-entry box model, the core–shell study and my lab instrumentation.

Direction 03

Research-grade aerosol optics for every lab

Turn my instrumentation experience into novel atmospheric instruments, starting with a low-cost, research-grade integrated photoacoustic nephelometer, so every experimental aerosol lab can measure absorption and scattering.

Builds on: resonator modeling, the laser-drift correction and my tutorial.

Direction 04

Aerosols in extreme-weather prediction

Learn how extreme weather is predicted and what today’s models miss, and test whether more realistic aerosols, through their effects on radiation, clouds and precipitation, can improve those predictions.

Builds on: Direction 01 and my radiative transfer work.
